### **The Limits of "I" and "Mine"**

If our feelings are kept in proportion, life becomes very beautiful. "Aham" (Ego) and "I" should only exist as much as necessary.

Imagine a person who, at every single step, says, "I built this myself, I did this myself." Every time someone visited his house, he would only start talking about this. Whenever people went there, he would say the exact same thing:

> *"Look at this table, I brought it myself. These chairs, I went to America recently, and I brought them myself."*

After listening to this constantly, someone finally said, *"Man, just tell us once! You brought everything, we get it, now please listen to us a bit."*
He replied, *"Not all of it, but **how** I brought it is what’s very important!"*

He then added, *"Just for a cup of tea, I have to listen to all of this..."* Why is he giving him coffee? Simply because he listens to his talk! It’s almost like a corruption lunch.

After listening to him endlessly, a guest was heading out and met another person who asked, *"Where are you going?"*
He replied, *"I am going to his house."*
The guest warned, *"Don't go there! I was so peaceful, and now my head is aching from listening to him over and over."*

### **The Illusion of Achievement**

Some egos are exactly like this. Just repeating the same thing constantly. What great thing could we have possibly achieved in this world?

Think about it: you invite someone for a meal and seat them. If the people who cooked start saying, *"Look at this soup, listen to everything we put in it! Every dish here, I made it myself. I made this curd myself, I made this milk myself, I did all of this myself. What do these guests even know?"*

The house owner sits there and thinks, *"What do these people know? I am the one running everything in this house."* If someone starts telling their guests this, how can that food ever taste good?

### **When Ego Crosses Its Limits**

Sometimes, when this feeling crosses its limits and grows wildly, that itself becomes the problem. Now, if you go to Dharwad, there is a psychiatric center there. There is one here in Miraj too. You should just go and observe; instead of seeing anything else, you should see that.

How does a patient sit there? He sits like a king. A true Maharaja. As soon as you go inside, he asks:
*"Why have you come? What do you want? Submit an application."*
He truly believes he is a king. *"Tell me what you want, take a thousand acres of land!"*

He sits there with his head full of this delusion. *"I am a Maharaja."* When the ego grows that much—when it becomes excessive—they can't be kept at home anymore. They are taken and kept there with the assurance, *"You are a very great person, please stay right here."*

We all have small, everyday egos at home. But if a massive ego takes over, that’s where you end up. Right? *"All this is mine, this whole town is mine, this entire street is mine! If I weren't here, how would this town even survive?"* If someone speaks like this, they take him straight to Dharwad.

### **The Weight of the Crown**

This "Aham", this "I", should only be exactly as much as necessary. Just because a crown is beautiful, if you wear a ten-kilo crown and sit, what will happen to you?

How do they make real crowns? Very light. The actual gold in it isn't what matters; they paint it a gold color, and it looks grand, which is perfect. Otherwise, if they placed kilos of pure gold on your head and asked you to sit for an hour... you couldn't.

Similarly, ego is fine, but only as much as is needed. If that "I" grows excessively, it destroys the beauty and auspiciousness of life.

### **The Mountaineer and the Sparrow**

What is there to boast about in this world? Let me tell you a small incident.

A great mountaineer once decided, *"I must climb to the very peak of a mountain that no one in this world has ever climbed."* It's not wrong to climb. So, he started his journey. After practicing and climbing for four to six years, he felt, *"There is no mountaineer as great as me."*

One day, he reached the very top of that peak and stood there. He declared:
*"I am the absolute first in mountaineering! I am standing at the highest place in the world. I am the very first human in history to do this!"*

Just then, a sparrow arrived. It flew in, sat right on his head, and said, *"I can be your witness if you want... How did you get here?"*
He asked the sparrow in shock, and it casually replied, *"Hey, we come here every day! People like you come here, and we just hang out with you. We fly happily up here."*

The man asked, *"You fly this high?"*
The sparrow replied, *"I just got here now, didn't I? Besides, who else is with us? We didn't come wearing oxygen masks. We didn't come wearing heavy gear. We have nothing on our bodies, we just came flying up. Since you struggled all the way from the bottom, I guess I have to be your witness so you can go tell the world!"*

He stood there with the sparrow sitting on his head. No matter how many times that sparrow flew up there, its name never appeared in any newspaper. It was never hailed as "Number One." But his name did. Why? Because he brought a camera and took a selfie.

### **The Need for Proportion**

Life is grand. But constantly talking about oneself to artificially inflate one's glory makes Ego ("Aham") and Attachment ("Mine") grow massive. When they cross their limits, they start to ruin life.

Everything requires proportion. Take the human body—it has a specific form. The head should only be a certain size; the hands and legs should only be a certain size. Imagine a massive head with tiny limbs—how would that look? Or legs that are a kilometer long with a tiny head—would that look nice? Everything requires a balanced form.

If "I" and "Mine" are balanced, they yield good results and bring happiness. If they grow excessively, they become destructive. What is cancer? It’s simply cells growing excessively. Normal cells grow as much as needed and stop, but cancer cells just keep growing and growing.

If we want to live peacefully, the "I" and the "Mine" should only be as much as necessary. We need these feelings in every act of our life. If there is no "I" at all, how do we live? If there is no "Mine", why would we do anything? Because of this feeling of "Mine", we protect things, we water plants, we clean our homes, we paint our walls. All of this should exist, but only in moderation. If so, the glory of life increases.

### **Basavanna’s Wisdom: The Hearth and the Earth**

We shouldn't let things—whether it's our society or our religion—get to our heads too much. If it becomes excessive, it creates chaos.

Light is necessary, but it shouldn't be so glaring that it blinds you. If it’s so bright you can’t even open your eyes, what use is it? It should be gentle. Look at a mild light—how beautiful, peaceful, and cool it is. When things are balanced, our experiences become rich.

But if it goes out of control, remember the words of Basavanna:

> *"If there is a fire in the stove, there is no problem. If the hearth catches fire, you can stand it; but if the earth itself catches fire, who can stand it?"*

Basavanna also said:

> *"If the dam drinks the water, if the fence grazes the field, if the wife steals in her own house, who else can I complain to, O Lord of Kudalasangama?"*

An embankment is built to hold water—what if it starts drinking it? A fence is put up to protect the crop—what if the fence itself gets up and starts grazing on the field? You wake up in the morning to find the crop is gone because the fence ate it. How ironic! If the very people supposed to protect the house start stealing from it, what happens then?

### **The Blossom of Life**

If the hearth burns, food is prepared. Every house has a stove and a fire, and that is good; it helps us live comfortably. But if the house itself catches fire, if the very earth we stand on catches fire, what is left?

Similarly, our ego—our "I" and "Mine"—should only be as big as the fire in a stove. If it sets your whole life on fire, it is terrifying. It becomes like the dam drinking the water or the fence grazing the field.

If life is kept balanced and pleasant, living is beautiful. The moment feelings become unruly, destruction begins. Feelings should blossom, yes, but when a flower blooms, does one of its petals stretch all the way to the next town? No, it only grows as much as it needs to. Take any flower—it retains its natural shape, and that is exactly where its true glory lies.
