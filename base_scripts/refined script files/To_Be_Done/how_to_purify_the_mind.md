It happens—a house gets dirty, whether big or small. No matter whose house it is, no matter how grand a house it is, it gets dirty. No matter how intelligent a person's mind is, sometimes it gets dirty.

It happens. Someone takes a very clean bath... using soap from France, a towel from England, wiping with a cloth brought from Italy, and applying perfume brought from Switzerland... he has done everything. He bathes systematically and comes out, wanting to remain clean just like that.

Is that possible?

As soon as he steps out, the fragrance starts fading, the body gets a little dirty, clothes get soiled... everything happens. After wandering all over the town, when he comes back home in the evening, he is dirty again. He bathes again. This is how it is.

That is why cleaning oneself day after day is called *Nitya Karma* (daily duties). Cleaning every day is called *Nitya Karma*. The world cannot be made permanently clean all at once. People say, "We must reform the world." Go ahead, do it as long as you are here. After you are gone, it gets dirty again, someone else comes, and they do their part—that's all. Keep doing it; everything cannot be cleaned up once and for all.

That is why, with our body and mind, what should we do? Look at good things, hear good things, do good things. After that, keep the mind clean, wishing well for everyone.

If you do this much, the mind becomes clean, and the body and senses become clean too. What a fine work this is! Just do this. Do this.

### The Story of Buddha and the Dancer

During the time of the Buddha... back then, there was a woman.

Buddha was sitting peacefully. His teachings were so calm, peaceful, as if floating. Not a single word of his would disturb anyone's mind—Buddha, of this land of India, spoke such beautiful, gentle, and peaceful words.

Just like Mahavira, just like Yajnavalkya, so was Buddha.

As Buddha sat quietly, people came there for his *darshan* (blessed view).

Among those who came was a very prominent person of that town—a woman. A grand dancer of extraordinary beauty, with a voice just as sweet. She was the dancer of that town.

For a moment, she felt a desire to see Buddha. While everyone was going to visit Buddha, she thought, "Why shouldn't I go too?" Until then, what had she done? Nothing much—she had filled the minds of all the young men in town with impurity. She had stirred up everyone's mind. Such was her influence!

One day, she desired to go: "Let me go and look at Buddha for a moment. What is this life, always being like this? Why shouldn't I try something different? Until now, I have only looked at ordinary men. From now on, why shouldn't I look at a Buddha?" What a beautiful thought! What a fine thought!

She went near Buddha. Buddha was sitting quietly and peacefully. Peace means Buddha; Buddha means peace. Just like you are sitting quietly right now—this is Buddha, the Buddha state.

Being peaceful... no lust in the eyes, no anger in the mind. No desire, no aversion—cool and calm, like a plant kept here. It is cool. Does it make any noise? Day and night, it stays right here. Green leaves growing... it gives joy to everyone, yet it remains calm. It does nothing—peaceful.

That was Buddha. Buddha is nothing else; being like this is Buddha, that's all. Right now, in this moment, all of you are Buddhas.

As Buddha sat like that, she went—the dancer. She went and said, *"Buddham Sharanam Gacchami"* (I take refuge in the Buddha). She bowed to Buddha. Buddha simply smiled gently. What beauty she had! But that beauty did not taint Buddha's eyes. Her sweet words did not pollute his mind; he remained peaceful. That is Buddha.

Then she asked, "Lord Buddha, I have come for your *darshan*. Please, out of compassion, save me. My eyes have become impure. My mind has become impure. For the last 10 to 15 years, my entire mind has been filled with impurity. Lord, is it possible for me to become pure again?"

She told Buddha, "I am now about 30 years old. Since I was 15 or 16, when I started dancing, for all these years—10 to 15 years—I never met someone like you. It never occurred to me that there could be a truth so beautiful and pure in this world. I sang, I danced, I disturbed people's minds... I did all of that. My life has become very impure, Lord Buddha. Is it possible for me to become clean?"

Buddha spoke calmly. He said softly, "Look, you must live in this present moment. Whatever has happened in the past up until now, simply forget it. Forget it. What is done is done; why revive all of that again? It will subside and be forgotten on its own."

"Imagine muddy water in a pot. Rain came, and the river water became muddy; they brought such muddy water and kept it here. The water is full of mud, slushy and dirty inside. Afterward, if they keep it down quietly like this, what happens?" Buddha asked, "What happens if it is kept like that?"

She replied, "Slowly, the mud settles at the bottom."

"And how does the water become?"

"The water becomes pure and clear."

"Similarly, do not shake it. The mud goes to the bottom, the water on top becomes clear. But if you think it hasn't become clear yet and shake it again, it becomes dirty all over again. Do not disturb it from below. What happened in the past should not be recalled."

### The Power of the Present Moment

What is done, is done. The past is to be forgotten, because it is not there to come again.

Will the past ever return? It is gone and finished. It will never return; it is gone. Right now, what you have is clarity—pure clarity.

Notice how clear you are in this very moment; do not recall the past. You only need to know how to remain clear, that's all. Just keep still. How do you make water clear? Do you do something to it? Do people perform rituals, penance, or churn it inside to make water clear? No.

Buddha said, "Just leave it quiet. Wait a little, that's all. Wait a little; it settles down on its own. If you recall the past, the mud rises again. If you live without recalling the past, and keep doing good from today, life gets cleared up and purified." What a simple remedy, and how practical! Why look back?

If we keep reminding ourselves of everything we did, our mind gets disturbed. Even regarding others, thinking about what they did—not just in this life, but in past lives... Our curiosity is such that we ask, "What were we in our past life?" Supposing you were something, what will you do with that information? Suppose you were a king or a beggar—whatever you were, what can anyone do with it now?

That is why God sent us here making us forget it, so we can remain calm. What will you do knowing it? What is done, is finished. So do not say, "Yesterday I was dirty, yesterday I was bad." Do not keep saying, "Yesterday those people were bad." Yesterday was yesterday. Learning to live today—that is *Dhyana Yoga*, Buddha's path of meditation. That is what is called *Vipassana*. Today, we have to live in this moment, and see whatever is good here, whatever is pleasant.

What beautiful words! Look at whatever is good. Listen to whatever is good. Do whatever is good. Why bring the past and pour it over yourself?

If I scolded you yesterday, or you scolded me, we were enemies. Today, as soon as we get up, if I forget it and you forget it, we are friends. Can anything from the past be corrected now? Tell me, what can you do? What is done is done. Can anything be done about it now? Nothing can be done. Just let it go.

How many times we have fallen, risen, made mistakes, misspoke... can all of that be brought back and corrected now? All of that is gone with the past. Forget all of it, and let us all become new again. Today, let us all become fresh and new.

None of us are poor. We are rich! Our mind is rich. Our body is rich. We have no enemies; everyone is our well-wisher.

### The Transformation

That woman—upon listening to those words—said, *"Buddham Sharanam Gacchami"* (I take refuge in the Buddha). "Lord Buddha, *"Dhammam Sharanam Gacchami"* (I take refuge in the Dhamma). Your teaching has filled my mind; that itself is Dhamma. *"Sangham Sharanam Gacchami"* (I take refuge in the Sangha). Now Buddha has arisen in my mind, Buddha's principles have arisen, and I have joined Buddha's fold," she said. That very woman became a great, wise person.

How wonderful!

Afterward, she returned home and said, "All of this belongs to Buddha. If anyone wants to enter this house, say:
* 'Buddham Sharanam Gacchami'
* 'Sangham Sharanam Gacchami'
* 'Dhammam Sharanam Gacchami'."

What a transformation!

In just a single moment, Buddha completely transformed such a person. Just one moment! Otherwise, if you list every mistake from the past and start doing penance for this much here, this much there... life will end, your lifespan will be gone.

If I forget everything about you, and you forget everything about me, and we become entirely new people, that is when the true *Deepavali* (festival of lights) begins in life.

The lamp of peace begins to burn in the mind. The lamps of peace and happiness begin to glow in the world. That is what is needed.

This is cleanliness. This is pure. God is an extraordinarily pure entity; God is never spoiled.

*"Yesham tvanta-gatam papam jananam punya-karmanam"*—people who perform good deeds... Good deeds do not just mean giving and taking; it means what you see, what you hear, what you do, what you think, what you read, what you write... everything. In every step of life, having peace and purity in whatever work we do—that is all.

If we do just that much, what a transformation happens! Life itself changes.

*"Punya-karmanam, tesham tvanta-gatam papam..."* How beautifully it is said!
*"Te dvandva-moha-nirmukta bhajante mam dridha-vrataḥ"*

And remember one thing: he says you must be strong in mind. Having done this today, do not go back to doing something else tomorrow. Let the mind remain firm: "I must remain pure"—keep this conviction in your mind.

Whatever happens, remain pure.

Whose house is dirty is not important; that *I* become clean is what matters most. What happens with us is we think, "I am so clean, but all these people are dirty. I don't drink, but all these people drink." While those who drink forget about it, if I keep thinking, "So many people drink," then even without drinking, that thought has entered my head more than it has affected them!

Likewise, the mistakes and flaws of the world are not what matter—*I* must remain pure, that's all.
