
**0:11** – There was a man who owned a large house. He had everything in his home required to live comfortably. Yet, he felt a sense of inner lack, and a dream arose within him.

**0:33** – His dream was: *"No one in this entire town should be as wealthy as me."* It seemed like a grandiose dream. He went further: *"No one in this entire country of India should appear as rich as me. I must become that wealthy."*

**0:57** – Such thoughts cross our minds at times, and that is how our hands begin to get dirty. You understand what "getting one's hands dirty" means, don't you? What we earn through our rightful hard work is clean. Whatever we seek or grab beyond that is dirty. Whatever we earn through effort is clean; whatever we crave beyond that for free is dirty. Thus, our hands keep getting dirtier—and this desire is the reason.

**1:31** – Tell me, who ever feels *"This is enough now"*? Earlier, a wage was 10 rupees; now it is not ten thousand, but a lakh rupees. Does anyone ever feel it is enough? The land used to yield a certain amount, now it yields much more—does anyone say it is enough?

**1:57** – If I am sitting on a chair right now, and someone places a new silver chair, will anyone say no to it? If a golden chair is placed, will the silver one still appeal? Try placing three chairs, and see if I sit on the basic one instead of the finest.

**2:18** – That is how the human mind operates: *"It isn't mine, someone else placed it here, I don't even fully own it. Yet, merely sitting on it creates such obsession. Imagine what more could be obtained!"*

**2:33** – This state is called ***Trishna***—an insatiable craving. What is present never gives satisfaction; one continuously longs for what is absent. This constant craving is *Trishna*.

**2:46** – That man had a grand dream. He declared: *"I must be the richest man in this country."* A dream is fine, but how does one fulfill it? If he has four acres now, he wants to own more acres than anyone else in the entire nation.

**3:25** – He realized this could not be achieved through mere daily labor; it had to be acquired effortlessly. Notice the difference in words: earning through labor versus acquiring. Acquiring means getting something for free—like winning a lottery.

**3:43** – People wish for wealth to fall into their laps. But wealth in a lottery doesn't come out of thin air; for one person to win, ten others must lose. Ten people become poorer so that one person becomes wealthy. Lottery organizers don't pay out of their own pockets—they collect from thousands and distribute to a few.

**4:09** – So he set out to acquire immense wealth effortlessly. Where to go? He thought: *"There are yogis, saints, and perfected beings (siddhas) who possess spiritual powers. If I go to one and receive a blessing, I will instantly become rich."*

**4:32** – He went searching and arrived at an ocean shore. Near the shore lived a sage, a deeply realized man of great wisdom. The man approached him and pleaded: *"O Holy One, I am very poor."*

**4:48** – That is how it started: *"I am very poor."* In reality, he had a house, land, and every material comfort. He was a prominent figure in his village, yet he claimed to be poor. There was no shortage of physical goods; there was only an emptiness in his inner contentment.

**5:26** – He begged the Guru: *"I am very poor. Bless me so that I become rich. Give me a mantra, grant me a blessing, wish it for me—I must become wealthy."*

**5:41** – Isn't that how human nature is? If someone offers free gifts tomorrow at 6 AM, who will stay to listen to a spiritual discourse? Everyone would rush for the gifts. The moment material greed starts, wisdom is forgotten.

**6:10** – The sage, being a man of supreme wisdom, said: *"Is this such a big matter? Do as I tell you, and I will make you as wealthy as you desire. You can become as rich as you wish."*

**6:29** – The man asked what he must do. The sage replied: *"Come tomorrow morning. You have 12 hours—from sunrise until sunset. Along this seashore, there are smooth, beautiful pebbles. Collect them throughout the day. Before the sun sets, bring them to me, and I will transform all those pebbles into precious gems."*

**7:10** – Imagine his joy! Each gem would be worth lakhs. With 12 hours available, he thought he could gather a massive heap and become world-famous for his wealth.

**7:35** – He arrived early the next morning, not wanting to waste a single minute. The Guru reminded him: *"Begin now, but remember, your time is strictly 12 hours. Whenever you feel satisfied that you have collected enough, come meet me at that very moment, and I will transform everything you gathered."*

**8:05** – The man went to the shore and began collecting smooth pebbles. Hours passed unnoticed. He didn't notice the sun rising high, nor did he feel hunger or thirst. He forgot everything, consumed by a single task: gathering pebbles.

**8:31** – He accumulated heaps upon heaps. Midday came, the sun beat down, and his stomach starved, but he paid no attention. Why? Because when one is consumed by hoarding, awareness vanishes. Age slips away without notice; exhaustion goes unfelt. Neglecting food, sleep, and well-being, he kept gathering.

**9:06** – He completely forgot the main instruction: that when he felt *enough*, he was supposed to return to the Guru and have the pebbles turned into gems. He forgot the goal while remaining lost in the act of collecting.

**9:23** – That is the nature of craving (*Kamana*), which is why it is called *Maya* (illusion). The mind kept telling him: *"Keep gathering! There is still time. Collect as much as possible—you won't get another chance tomorrow. This is your final opportunity."*

**9:57** – Driven by the thought that such an opportunity would never come again, he kept picking up more and more. He ran around all day, filling heaps, but he never reached the point of feeling *"This is enough."* The Guru had said to return when he felt content, but contentment never arrived.

**10:27** – The sun began to dip toward the horizon. As it touched the edge of the sky, one part of his mind urged: *"Hurry! Go back now! If the sun sets while you go fetch the Guru, everything is lost!"* But another part of his mind argued: *"There are still five minutes left. Why not pick up four more pebbles in these last few minutes? Why leave them behind?"*

**10:49** – While he ran around trying to collect four more pebbles, the sun set.

**11:03** – He ran back to the Guru in desperation: *"Guru, come quickly!"* The Guru replied: *"The sun has set. It is over. What you gathered over there—those ordinary pebbles—that is your only wealth."*

**11:19** – This is a symbolic story for all of humanity. Everyone's mind works in this exact manner. We constantly believe, *"If I achieve just this much more, my life will be complete,"* but it never becomes complete—because the material world itself is incomplete.

**11:36** – We think we can fix or satisfy everything around us. By the time you try to resolve four issues, eight new ones appear. Who all can you fix? As you sweep dust on one side, the wind blows dust onto another.

**12:05** – Seeing this world's incompleteness and constantly multiplying our cravings is the root cause of all sorrow. This state is called ***Bhava Malinya***—the pollution of inner sentiment and contentment. When inner purity is polluted by insatiable thirst (*Trishna*), true peace is lost.
