In the country of Greece, there lived a man named Socrates.
Named Socrates... a very calm and composed man.
He was a very great saint... a saint named Socrates.
Just like how we had Tukaram and others here, he was a very great saint.
What wisdom he had, such extraordinary wisdom.
He was an ordinary man.
Just a simple person who earned a living by doing simple stone-carving work.

Such a man's wife, inside the house...
You should see how enjoyable his life was.
His wife... a troublesome wife. Her name was Xanthippe, a quarrelsome woman.
Though she had such a sweet name, Xanthippe...
Socrates' wife, Xanthippe...
If she so much as looked at Socrates, she felt like beating him, throwing water on him, or throwing dirty dishwater on him.
Because he didn't earn anything or bring anything home; he only came home to eat—that's how he was.
However, when he went outside and stood, thousands upon thousands of people would listen to his words and feel immense joy—such was his wisdom.

He transformed the minds of thousands of people.
He transformed ordinary young people into intelligent, wise youth.
He was one of the greatest philosophers in the world—Socrates.
His name is eternal.

To such a man, someone came... a young man came.
That young man was about 25 years old.
He had been married for a year.
He had chosen his wife and married her out of his own choice.
He had fallen in love and married her.
It was a marriage of mutual liking—you must know, not just an arranged marriage, but a marriage of love.
Meaning, both of them had liked each other.
They had decided, "Let us spend our life of a hundred years beautifully together," and got married.

A year passed.
After that, the young man came to Socrates... came to Socrates.
He said, "I don't know why, but I feel like I don't want this life anymore."
"Why?" asked Socrates.
There was sadness on his face, wasn't there? "Why? You should be happy! You are only 25 years old, you have a good body, you have intelligence, you have received plenty of education. Why are you not happy? Why is there sadness on your face?" asked Socrates, the old man.

Then the young man said, "What happiness is left, noble sir?"
"All happiness is gone."
"From now until I die, I will live in sadness, I will live in misery. Look at what has happened," he told Socrates.
"Tell me, what can be done? I married out of love, and now I realize she is not good."
"She isn't the way I want her to be."
"Only now have I come to realize that she has no beauty."
"To live with such a person for so many years until I die... to keep someone in the house looking at whom brings no happiness to the heart... to spend 40, 50, 60 years like this... my life is ruined," he said.

"Please tell me some solution or remedy for this," he asked Socrates.
Socrates smiled and said,
"Oh, that is a very simple solution. I will tell you one thing, just do that," he said. "Your problem will be solved. Your whole life will become full of happiness," said the saint.
Now, you might be thinking, "Will he advise him to get a divorce?"

Socrates said,
"Before getting married, did you get to know her?" he asked. "Yes, I did."
"We got married out of mutual love and liking."
"What did you see in her that made you like her?" he asked.
"I saw her and liked her," he said.
"Is what you saw and liked in her not there anymore?" he asked.
Where could it go?

"Is she still the same, or has she changed?" he asked.
"No, she is still the same."
"Then, if the person you liked a year ago hasn't changed, but you feel this way, then the one who has changed must be you—look within," he said.

"Your mind must have changed."
"Your perspective has changed."
"The perspective through which you once saw her as beautiful—you have lost that perspective."
"Now, what you must do is retrieve that same perspective through which you once liked her."
"That is within your hands. Look at her again with those same eyes," he said.
Socrates said, "Go now, look at her as if she is new to you. Look as if you haven't married yet."
"If she looked so beautiful before marriage, she must be even more wonderful now. Go!"
"Look at her with the same perspective you had back then. Look at how good she is—what a complexion, what beauty, what words, what a musical voice she has! How wonderful! Look at how much she loves you," he said.

The young man came back after about eight days.
His face was glowing with joy. He didn't come alone; he brought her along with him!

"How are you?" asked Socrates.
"Oh Master! I don't even know how a hundred years will pass by now," he said.
"There is so much joy!"
"My wife is so beautiful; there is no one like her in this entire world," he said.

Just eight days earlier, he had come saying his life was ruined.
Did anyone change? No one changed. The wife didn't change; she remained as she was.
What changed? The vision of his eyes changed, that's all.
His mind changed.
And the work of bringing about that change was done by a saint like Socrates.

If someone had come to us with such a problem, we would have said, "Why bother? Just get a divorce!"
"If you don't want it, leave it!"

We break things apart.
Socrates is one who establishes truth...
and that too, inside the heart.

Life doesn't get ruined on its own. If a little bitterness enters the feelings held within the heart, then life gets ruined.
As long as there is sweetness in your feelings, the world is happy and gives happiness.
If feelings become bitter, then the world will not feel sweet to us.
There should be sweetness inside the heart.
There should be no bitterness. That is the whole point.

Whether in your gaze, in your heart, in the actions you perform, or in the words you speak—if that sweetness fills them, life becomes heaven, full of peace and comfort.
That is within our own hands...
within our own hands.
We must attain that.
