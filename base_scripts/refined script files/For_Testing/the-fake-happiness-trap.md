### **The Wealth of a Smile**

A smiling face does wonders. People who possess it might not have anything else, but they have a pleasant face. They are always smiling, keeping themselves and others happy. Wherever they go, everyone welcomes them. Who would invite someone with a grumpy, frowning face? Tell me, what kind of wealth is that? A smiling face is itself a profound wealth.

A few smiling faces—that is true wealth. All these are treasures given to us by nature. Why do we need to spend money on this? Has anyone ever gone to college to learn how to smile?

Yet, nowadays, they conduct "laughing classes" in some places. For whom? For the very big, wealthy people. You can't teach this to little children; they already know how to smile. You can't teach it to villagers either; they naturally laugh, cry, and express themselves freely.

### **The Irony of Laughing Clubs**

They conduct these classes for the elite who live in massive houses. If you go to Mumbai, Chennai, or Bengaluru, you will see them—**Laughing Clubs**. They charge a fee of 100 rupees a day just to laugh. How ridiculous is that fee? You really must go and see how funny it is!

Big officers and prominent people gather and sit silently in a ground. A man comes to make them laugh. He doesn’t even tell a joke; he just says, *"One, two, three, laugh!"* (Isn't that hilarious? You wouldn't know unless you've seen it). It's incredibly funny. They laugh. Then he says, *"Hey, laugh louder! One, two..."*

The body has its own natural rhythm. Why should I laugh just because someone commands it? I should laugh when I feel true happiness. As soon as you put a count on it, how can a genuine laugh emerge? He makes them forcefully laugh twice, collects his 100 rupees, and as he walks away with the cash, he chuckles to himself, thinking, *"What fools these people are!"*

Right? Smiling is a wonderful, natural wealth.

### **Discovering Our Inner Treasures**

Similarly, everything is a treasure. Inside each of us, there is hidden wealth. Expressing that out in the open is what we call *Satsanga* (good company/spiritual gathering). We have these treasures within, and we need to let them bloom.

Everyone has one thing or another. The wealth of life is present in everyone. If you have legs to walk, isn't that wealth? If you lose both legs, what happens to your wealth? Half your wealth is gone. You have two hands—aren't they wealth? If those two are gone, life just breaks into pieces. If you still have eyes to see, that brings joy. If those are gone, where is the wealth?

There are highly educated, "big" people who can't walk or work with their hands. They just acquire massive degrees, put up a nameboard, and sit there. What do they do? If anyone approaches them, they speak harshly. They don't even have basic manners. They may have read and written a lot, but one must also know how to speak gracefully.

### **Knowledge Without Compassion**

That is why they put up boards calling themselves *Pandits* (Scholars). When walking past their house, people avoid even looking in that direction. *"Hey, Pandits live there, keep moving,"* they say. This means these scholars are not adjusting or welcoming. If you accidentally end up at their door, their immediate question is, *"Why did you come? What do you want?"*

Instead, true speech should be welcoming: *"Come, come in! I am so happy you came. Tell me, how can I serve you?"* If you speak like this, a guest will never want to leave. Imagine offering them a cup of coffee and lovingly asking, *"Where did you come from? Why did you visit?"* They will go everywhere praising you, saying, *"What scholars! Such wonderful people!"*

But instead, as soon as you arrive, they pick a fight. *"Why haven't you applied sandalwood paste? Why haven't you worn this on your neck? Where is the scripture (Shastra) that allows this?"* What happens to the person who visits them? The scholar says, *"One shouldn't even look at faces like yours."* And the visitor thinks, *"Seeing your face today was my biggest mistake."*

Where is the smile in this? Speech itself should smile.

### **The Beauty of Simple Words**

Villagers speak so beautifully. Even without formal grammar or respectful suffixes (*Re*), look at how they talk. They simply say, *"Come man, come to our house, eat something and go."* There is so much feeling in those simple words. *"Come man, you haven't visited in a while. Just drop by for a day, have a meal with us, and then go."* What grammar is needed for this?

But the grammarian will sit there analyzing: *"That sentence is wrong, this word shouldn't be used like that."* The villager will eventually think, *"Why did I even speak to him?"*

The word that comes directly from the heart contains beauty—that is true wealth.

### **The True Meaning of Sadhana (Spiritual Practice)**

Just as there is wealth in our legs and hands, there is wealth in our face and mind. There is wealth in the heart and the head. We must bring all of this out. Expanding and expressing these inner treasures is what we call **Sadhana** (Spiritual Practice).

Such practice is *Adhyatmika Sadhana* (Spiritual realization). It means realizing what is inside us and using it to enrich the world. Only then do we become truly rich. True wealth is internal. There are three types of this richness:

1. **Emotional Richness** (*Bhava Srimantike*)
2. **Knowledge Richness** (*Jnana Srimantike*)
3. **Action/Deed Richness** (*Karya Srimantike*)

We shouldn't live like poor people. We must live richly through our work, our words, our good intentions, and our knowledge.

### **Life as an Art**

Consider the international art markets abroad. They bid exorbitant prices for paintings. Sometimes, a simple yet artistically profound picture is priced in crores. A person pays crores of rupees just to take that picture home. And the artist who drew it might have nothing; they just scribbled something from their soul.

It’s surprising. It proves that wealth is in our hands, in our bodies. The buyer might not even understand that it’s the most valuable picture; they just buy it, bring it home, and hang it on a wall. Earning money is an entirely different skill. The artist may not know how to make money, but the businessman does.

Some people build massive, beautiful houses in a short time. Not by struggling, but by living a wonderfully beautiful life, saving diligently, and working hard. Everyone who sees it feels happy.

Then there are those who use their retirement pension to build a rented house. And what are these rented houses like? Sometimes there is water, sometimes not. Sometimes there is electricity, sometimes not. But why build a house after retirement? **What does retirement mean?** It means you are no longer the "owner." He builds a house, transfers it to the next generation, and ends up living like a tenant in his own home.

### **The Ultimate Masterpiece**

How should we live? Living itself is an art. Practicing that art is *Sadhana*.

All of you sitting here right now peacefully—this is an art. Waking up in the morning, observing the world, and filling your mind with happiness—isn't this an art? Right now, in this very town, many households are fighting. Coffee isn't made, tea isn't ready, they woke up late, the water hasn't boiled, the power is out. They are scolding someone because the water didn't come.

While all that chaos happens there, we are sitting here happily. What is missing? Joy is present. **Life is an art. Everything is an art.**
