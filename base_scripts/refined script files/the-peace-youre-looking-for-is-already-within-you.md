### The Lives of Great Devotees

Within this very land, you must look at the kind of people who have lived and passed on. Day or night. Four *javas* (periods) of the day, four *javas* of the night. Didn't they say it? Right here, in one of your towns, there lived a beautiful life, a man who led a beautiful existence named **Havinala Kallayya**.

Havinala is a town on the banks of the Bhima river. There, a great person named Kallayya lived. Look at his words. An ordinary man. A man who simply did the work of a goldsmith.

While doing his goldsmith work, he thinks to himself: *"Nobody told me, nobody asked me, why shouldn't my life also become somewhat like gold?"* That is the feeling. It doesn't happen like that for us. We only think about when we will become rich from this gold. *"Life itself should become gold,"* said Havinala Kallayya. Looking at the gold, the feeling that he should save some of it—for Havinala Kallayya, it was, *"This is my life."*

He made it so sweet, so wonderful. He says:

> "Wherever I look, there I see only You."

Isn't it beautiful?

> "Waking up, I see only You. Waking from sleep, I see only You. Oh, place your meditation in the night, Mahalinga Kalleshwara."

Look at the way he lived. Where did his life reach? *"Wherever I look, what I see there is only You."* What is this? These are all forms of God. He had reached this beautiful state.

### The Vision of Havinala Kallayya

A boy. But what a wonder! 900 years ago, in a simple town, a simple village with no facilities, look at the life he lived. That Kallayya, you must see.

* *"Wherever I cast my eyes, whatever is visible there, I see only You in it."*
* *"Wherever I look, there I see only You."*
* *"Wandering around, I see only You."*
* *"Waking up in the morning, I see only You."*
* *"Waking from sleep, I see only You."*

Not just that, *"Oh, place your meditation in the night, Mahalinga Kalleshwara."* This was his life.

What kind of people have lived! Their names aren't very famous, but what words that boy spoke! What beauty. *"I desire nothing. Wherever I look, whoever it may be. Be it women, men, the elderly, ordinary people, the educated, the uneducated, the rich, the poor. No question. Wherever I look, God, I must see only You."*

*"Wherever I cast my eyes in this world, there, your vision must happen. I see only You."* It's not just a wish; *"I am seeing it like that."* He reached this state. Who? Kallayya. His God is Mahalinga. What a beautiful feeling. This is the way to smoothen life, the moral path.

### The Devotion of Kadire Remmavve

There was another woman in this same land. Her name was **Remmavve**. You might have heard of her. Her work was to extract yarn using a spinning wheel (*Raati/Kadira*). This was her job. Is this a very big job? Will millions of money come from this? She just kept spinning it all day, turning the wheel. She was extracting the yarn.

As she was extracting it, listening to the sound that came from it, her mind became absorbed. A thread was coming from her hand, and her mind was filled with that sound. **"Kadire Remmavve"** (Remmavve of the Spindle). As the spindle turned, the sound that arose when it turned—the feeling of *Omkara* came through that sound. It went straight into her head. She spun the yarn, but her mind was completely filled with *Omkara*.

*"Why do I spin the yarn? To wrap it? No, to feel the joy. To feel the joy."*

> "The yarn was spinning, the spindle was filling."

That is her *vachana* (vow/saying). The yarn spun, the spindle filled, life ended. Our job is to spin the yarn, to fill the spindle. We must spin; the yarn was spinning, the spindle was filling. Our yarn shouldn't tear, our yarn shouldn't spoil. It shouldn't be too much or too little. That spindle must be filled, it must be spun. And then it must be filled. At the last moment, there must be a feeling of having filled it. That was her spiritual practice.

---

### Cultivating a Fulfilled Mind

What kind of people have lived! Just spinning the spindle and making life beautiful—a woman. The spindle filled, the yarn spun. Isn't this beautiful?

We say this while lying on the bed: *"From morning to evening, the yarn spun. Lying down at night, the spindle filled."* Put a full stop there; *"My life became meaningful, it filled,"* close your eyes. Don't close them forever! Every day... every day, we must live like this. The whole day the spinning wheel must turn, the yarn must spin, the spindle must fill. In the final moment, the final thread should be spun and it should be full. This is a life that hasn't become empty. A life that has filled with good deeds, with goodness, whatever life fills up, that is a fulfilled life.

Our minds must be filled like a garden. If we look inside, we should see beautiful flowers, trees, good people... we shouldn't see useless things.

It happens to our minds many times. As soon as we close our eyes, a Ravana comes. Look. Or some other guy comes. The Ramayana happens, the Mahabharata happens, everything happens inside... nothing outside, it's all prepared inside! If the mind becomes this corrupt, what is the meaning of living? asked Kadire Remmavve. *"Why should anyone care about what others say? We spin the spindle, spin the yarn, and fill the spindle."*

Our life's purpose is to spin the yarn. To spin every moment. Take a simple piece of cotton, turn it into a fine thread, and fill it. Until then, there is that sound, right? Within that sound is the existence of God. We must experience that existence of God, Kadire Remmavve said. Isn't this enough? This is **"Bhakti Shastrani Mananiyani"** (The scriptures of devotion are worthy of contemplation). We must keep such words in our minds.

### The Essence of True Practice

Did you get the two concepts?

1. The beautiful words of **Havinala Kallayya** (the man).
2. The teachings of **Kadire Remmavve** (the woman).

We need Kallayya's beautiful words, that is the scripture of devotion. What is it? Day or night, *"I only see the good, the good, the good, God, I must see it."* We must ask for the good. We must shape our lives like that. And after that, what Remmavve said: *"The yarn was spinning, the spindle was filling."*

We must keep such words of devotion in our minds, keep putting them in. Then the mind becomes full. The feeling of Mahalinga Kalleshwara begins. Life becomes sweet.

What happens to us is, instead of filling our minds with such words, we listen to whatever anyone says in this world. "Now I'm sitting here, let's see how he sits," they say. We say nothing. What if they said something from above? Or while walking? If we put all those things in our heads—we all experience this—if we put them in, how can we spin the yarn and fill the spindle? Just put good things in. Just forget the rest. What else can be done? Having come into the world, there will be honor, dishonor, people say good things, people say bad things. There are good people today, they might be bad tomorrow; today they are bad, tomorrow they become good. This is the world.

In such a world, we must keep filling our heads with good things. Look at all of you, such good people. You have worked hard to come all the way here and sit nicely, feeling happy. Shouldn't we be happy seeing this? But some guy says one thing, and we spoil our minds. We let all the good things get ruined by a single word. This goes against *"Bhakti Shastrani Mananiyani"*.

What we must do is say, *"Let it go."* How beautiful it is in Kannada. Not just let it go, let it play out and let it go. It should be like that. If there is no struggle in the world, what is the meaning of our practice? When difficulties come, we should face them. The stream should be full, we should jump and swim across. If it's empty, bone-dry, only ankle-deep water, and you are trying to swim in a dried-up lake—what a big deal is that? See? There must be opposition in this world. In the midst of that, there should be a Buddha. A Remmavve.

### Ignoring the Noise

Many people used to come to Remmavve's house. They came and told her all the gossip of the town. Then Remmavve would say:
*"Look, the yarn is spinning. The spindle is filling. I am hearing the sound."*
They would say, *"She is not in a state to listen."*
She replied, *"I am not in a state to listen to what you are saying."*

Let them be them. Our focus is spinning, pulling the yarn, and doing our work. Putting that divine sound in our minds and living happily is what matters.

You must have heard of **Surdas**. Poor man, he didn't have eyes to see. But look at his every moment... just songs and songs. You must read one verse of what Surdas wrote. It's so beautiful. His words have Krishna dancing in them. His words express the message of the Gita. Sweetness flowed in his words.

Look at Tulsidas's Ramayana, how beautifully it is written. Look at the kind of Dasas who have lived in Karnataka... just one line: *"Why do you need yourself, why do you need your way, if the strength of your Name is there, that's enough."*

There are thousands of Sharanas (mystic saints) here who have spoken such beautiful words. We must keep such words in our minds, keep absorbing them, and if we do, our minds will find peace and our problems will fade away.
