There was a tree. It was a tree in a backyard, and underneath that tree, there were chickens.

They were sitting on their eggs, hatching chicks.

One day, what happened was—there was an eagle, or a garuda. That eagle's egg accidentally fell there. Where? Near the eggs of these chickens. The egg that was laid up high fell due to the wind. Fortunately, it didn’t break.

It fell right next to these eggs. The chicken gathered it along with its own eggs and sat on it as well.

Then, one day, the eggs hatched. All the chicks came out, and so did this one—which one? The eagle.

Growing up alongside them, how was this eagle? It grew up walking around just like them—these chicks that were there, the chicken's chicks. Can they fly very high? If there was even a little noise, they would run and scramble to hide inside their basket.

Along with them, this one was also hiding—which one? The eagle.

But it didn't know: "I am an eagle."

One day, an eagle came—a big eagle came and perched high up on the tree. Because of the speed and force with which that eagle arrived, all the chickens ran away.

Big chickens, small chickens—everyone ran inside the house. Along with them, this one also started running—which one? The young eagle.

The big eagle looked down from above and was astonished: "Why is this one running inside? How did it end up here?"

After that, it flew down and caught it. Catching it, it placed the young eagle up on the tree. It placed it on the tree and asked: "Hey, why are you running over there?"

The young eagle started trembling: "Please let me go! Everyone else has gone inside, let me go, I will die!"

The big eagle said: "Hey, you are not a chicken. You are an eagle."

It replied: "How am I an eagle? I am a chicken. My mother is over there, my friends are over there, we all belong over there. Let me go, I am very scared! Wherever I was, let me go back there!"

Then the big eagle said: "Hey, inside you, there is an eagle! Look at the power you have! See how I fly into the sky? You have the exact same capability."

Then the young eagle said: "I didn't know that."

Because no one had taught it, living among chickens, it had become a chicken.

That is why, by being among cowards, we become cowards. When we stay with people who say "I can't do it," we simply become chicks ourselves.

Otherwise, why wouldn't we be able to do it? Why can't we?

It didn't know this—why? Because another eagle hadn't come along yet.

That eagle said: "Now I will push you, fly!"

When it pushed, the young eagle thought: "This tree is so big and high, if I fall down into the depth, my life is over, I will die!" It didn't even know how to fly; it had only walked around in the dirt heap.

When the big eagle pushed it: "Whether you live or die, spread your wings!"

When it spread its wings out of fear, it started floating in the air.

Then it realized: "Hey, I know how to float too! Why just float? I can rise to the heights of the sky!" And it soared.

That is how human beings are.

That is how humans are. Nothing is impossible. It is possible.

When people said, "Quit India!"—didn't they? They said, "Go away, leave and go!"

Then one man came—Tilak came. He declared: "Freedom is my birthright!" Thirty crore (300 million) Indians started saying, "Freedom is my birthright!"

What happened when one man stood up and awakened everyone? He awakened them just like the eagle did.

Similarly, when Mahatma Gandhi said, "Quit India!"—they left. How amazing! Just one statement, and thirty crore voices joined. What power!

If we sit on the ground believing "I cannot do this," who is more unfortunate than us? Destroying this weakness of the mind—that is *sadhana* (spiritual effort). Saying "I can't" is the weakness.

Even in the midst of poverty, one must find joy; such is our capability.

Are we going to wait to be peaceful only after becoming as rich as America? Where is that written?

Whatever nation we build, right here in our own village, in a small one-acre piece of land—we can grow wonderful crops, we can live joyfully. Who cares about Bangalore, who cares about Delhi?

If a person builds their mind like that, bringing forth the feeling that "It is possible," then that person grows.

That is why Patanjali says: "You are not ordinary. You are not ordinary—inside, you are unlimited."

Inside you, it is filled—how much is it filled? Divine nature is filled within you. You are not just a human being; you are God manifest. *Purusha* (the Supreme Being). God. *Tat Tvam Asi* (Thou Art That). You are the Divine itself—that much capability is within you.

Aurobindo Maharshi said: "O human, why do you think of yourself as small? Tell me. Rise, rise, rise, rise! Inside you, there is a wonderful consciousness."

> "The divine reality is hidden in you. The very purpose of life is to be conscious of the divine reality which is in thy heart."

An extraordinary capability, a divine power, O human, is within you. That divine nature is inside you. You can become God manifest, you can experience the Divine.

Do not think of yourself as ordinary: "I belong to a lower caste, I belong to a higher caste, we are women, we are men, we are villagers, we are poor..." Unless we remove all these thoughts from our minds, a human being cannot become an realized soul, and life cannot become extraordinary.

What is the obstacle to such a life? It is sitting with folded hands, saying, "I cannot do it."

Giving up and saying, "I cannot do it." Why can't you?

One must recognize that power within. Doesn't Basavanna say it? How is it? Inside a plant, sweet juice is filled. What a wonderful example Basavanna gives!

Inside a plant, sweet juice is filled—a small plant. As it grows, grows, and grows, sugarcane juice fills inside it. In the beginning, what is there? Nothing. There is no juice, just a simple leaf.

But after that, it starts filling up, and inside, it becomes all sugar, all sweetness!

Similarly, O human, at first it may feel like "I am very small," but grow and see—you are vast, you are divine, you are pure, you possess divine nature! That is what Aurobindo Maharshi says.

That is how we must grow. Removing the weakness that exists in our mind—to remove that weakness, we don't have to do much. We just need to light a lamp within: "It is possible for me." Self-confidence.

Self-confidence.

Look at a small oil lamp (*panate*). What is there in an oil lamp? How big is it? It's tiny. Yet it says: "O human, light me and see! Light me and see—I will push away this darkness, I will destroy it!"

Just a small lamp!

Not just for human beings, it spreads light even upon the Gods. "I dispel the darkness present in the world. Darkness does not have the power to destroy me. I have the power to push darkness away, but darkness does not have the power to extinguish me!"

That is how a human being should grow.

That is what Deepavali, the lamp, tells us: "So what if it is dark? Do not complain about the darkness. Do not say everything is bad. You shine, and a heaven will be created around you! Light will create light!"

What a lesson the lamp gives! We must feel it. We are not poor, nor are we helpless. We are nothing less—we are individuals possessing divine power.

When this feeling comes, a person becomes extraordinary.

Why shouldn't it happen? We will grow, we will build, we will live in joy. To laugh and live in this world doesn't require much.

To accumulate material things requires a lot, but what is needed to laugh? To be happy doesn't require much.

Nothing much is needed—a little food, a little water, clothes, the strength to work, and a few affectionate people around—that itself is heaven.

Heaven.

We must build such a life. There should be no darkness. There should be no darkness in the mind; there should be the light of joy, the light of hope, the light of knowledge. We are not lesser. We are people carrying a spark of the Divine.

When this feeling arrives, that is what true Yoga is.

How wonderfully Maharshi Patanjali speaks such extraordinary words: "O human, do not fear."

> *"Sa tu dirgha kala nairantarya satkara asevito dridhabhumih"*
> (Practice cultivated continuously, without interruption, and with devotion becomes firmly grounded.)

You will certainly reach it, but let your mind remain strong. Do not forget that it is possible.

How beautifully they say it! That is why we must leave behind the thought that we are small—leave it behind right now.

In our home, there is no darkness, only light. In our mind, there is no despair, only the light of hope, the light of knowledge. We are not lesser. We are people carrying the essence of God.

This feeling—this is what is important.
