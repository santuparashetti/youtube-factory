When the Buddha was about to pass away, a man lay down on a bed of straw, on stone straw, during his final moments, the very last moments of his life. Having wandered for eighty years across the entire country, walking continuously from early times, the Buddha had lived.

On his body were two *cheevars*, one *cheevar*. *Cheevar* means a robe; they call their robe a *cheevar*. It's a yellowish kind of robe. He carried an alms bowl, a staff, and a robe while wandering for eighty years.

Throughout that entire region—we call it *vihara*, the original word is *vihara*, which means the Buddha's wandering—he had traveled everywhere in that region.

When his final moments arrived, as he lay down on the straw beneath a tree just before shedding his body, people came running: "A man is coming."

His disciples were standing and sitting all around. He came running, panting. Tears welled up in everyone's eyes as the Buddha was about to give up his body. Then this man arrived. When he arrived, no one was making a way for him. He said, "I have come from so far away, I have come to understand," he said. Just as they were giving up their bodies, he had come to understand.

They, his disciples, asked, "What have you been doing for eighty years?"
Then he said, "What do I know about when these people will give up their bodies?"

"Anyway, whatever it may be, get out of the way," he brushed everyone aside, pushed past them, went right in front of the Buddha, bowed to the Buddha, and said, "I want to become a Buddha."
He gave a discourse: "I must become liberated, I must become a Buddha, I must become a *Kevali*, I must become myself," saying, "What should I do?"
"This moment must happen," he said.
Poor fellow, he was giving up his body, and this fellow had come to understand.

The Buddha smiled and said a very beautiful thing:
"Look, just look.
Listen, just listen.
Do, just do.
You must listen, you must not remain listening.
You must look, you must not remain looking.
You must do, you must not remain doing.
Just listen.
Just look.
Just do.
But do not say 'I will do,' do not say 'I will look,' do not say 'I will listen,' just be."

How wonderful that is! This was his final teaching.

That man felt so much joy; he was very rich, having abandoned all wealth to come there, traveling hundreds of kilometers on foot. The Buddha was passing away, and he had come just in time to become wise. He sat there, and as he sat, the man laughed.
"Laugh?"
"Why?"
"Everything is gone."

So easily he attained Buddhahood; the final man attained Buddhahood without ever meeting him before, but in that final moment, he realized, "I am pure, pure, pure, I am alone."
He just looked. He did not look as a man, nor as a woman, nor as a rich person, nor as a poor person, nor as a wise person, nor as an unwise person—he just looked.

He just looked. He himself had become peaceful. When a person just looks, they remain peaceful. If you look with any specific identity, trouble arises. If you look at household owners with some judgment of color or status, trouble arises. We go just to look—like gazing at the blue sky—what does it matter where a piece of dirt falls? Just look.

Why go out to look? Why did the sages or wise men of India travel? Just to look and keep moving. Don't say, "It is like this here, it is like that there." Just look. Just look at who is wearing a cap, who is wearing a scarf, who is like this, who is like that, who is limping, who is not—just look, and do not make any commentary.

That meditation is Buddha's meditation.
How wonderful, how beautiful—just look.

For just a single moment, just look, and you will realize who you are.
Just look: "I am the seer, not the object that I am seeing."
I am just the seer. What I am seeing is not me.
If you look like this, there is no attachment, no aversion; you just look.

For that, the remedy—there, their remedy, Patanjali also gives a remedy. They speak very wonderfully. Everyone, whichever saint you take, has given a remedy. A remedy to attain *Kevalatva* (aloneship/isolation).

In the end, you must become *Kevali*. That man, whatever wealth he came with, was the final Buddha. Many people were near the Buddha, but they hadn't become Buddhas yet; he came at the very end and became a Buddha. The Buddha closed his eyes, and this man became a Buddha.

How one should see: It doesn't take many moments; if the mind has intensity, it takes just a single moment; without intensity, there is nothing. That man had such intensity; he left everything, all his wealth, and traveled hundreds of kilometers the moment the news broke, running and running, shouting, "I must become liberated!"

How wonderful it is! He came, sat down, and listened to the very last words, becoming liberated. The Buddha gave his final teaching and departed.

That final teaching:
**Just look.
Just listen.
Just do.
Just live.
Just keep looking.**

This is not ours, this is not theirs—it's like that, just be.
Even now, if you look at me just like that, no headache will occur.
If you think, "These people belong to this party, these people belong to this caste, these people belong to this religion, these people are against us, these people are for us"—if you look like that, bodily ailments will start.
That is why it is called *samsara*. What is *samsara*? Seeing things and spoiling your head is *samsara*.
Causing trouble to yourself is *samsara*, remaining cool is the liberated state (*nis-samsara*).

Remaining cool—what happens in the world, we do whatever happens. Nature does its work, moving along wonderfully, creating various forms. In my view, this world does not run according to us; the world runs according to its own nature.

But for us, it's not like that: "I set it up this way, so everyone must follow it this way. I speak this way, so everyone must speak this way. I say this, so everyone must repeat it." If it's like that, your head will never settle.

You remain as you are, your family remains as they are, children remain as they are, animals remain as they are, people coming and going remain as they are—how wonderful that is!
There is peace; the house becomes cool.

Why is it like that? Because you are like that.
If I say you are *Advaiti* (non-dual), why are you *Dvaiti* (dual)? If you are of a certain caste, why are you of that caste? The world is just an echo—an opposing echo keeps happening.

Opposing echo—this echo is different. What I say, the opposite of it happens; the world is not like that, whatever I say, the opposite exists. It is an opposing echo. The world is an opponent; if I am like this, another is like that.

Even now, you read newspapers, you listen to speeches, one person says something and another refutes it. Why? Because it feels that way to us, we want to refute it. And one person has one kind of difficulty, another has another. Whose refutation is right? Both are just standing there—East and West never meet, and the conflict begins.

Thus, our state of being *Kevali* means just being.
Ah, Lord, you know all this, you are great.

And similarly, *Kevali*—*Kevali* means just being. Here, telling someone a teaching, leaving it there and going to another place, telling them something else—they used to argue, yes, they did. But after doing it, they forgot about it. Such a wonderful, like a living sculpture, that teacher was.

An advanced soul, a question is asked by someone, an ordinary person asks: "Sir, I am an ordinary person, why do you wander around like this, why don't you also settle down?"
And that teacher answers: "Look, you just eat coarse wheat, I eat something else, I eat joyfully, however it is, bliss is bliss, body maintained, mind controlled, transcending all illusions, cutting down the ties of *samsara*, planting the seed of the Brahma-bead..."

Everything is wonderful! Planting the seed of the Brahma-bead—the unbroken circle of existence, the breath as a chariot, flowing through the *sushumna* channel. When the five senses are restrained, at that time, in your garden, awareness blossoms, and the moon of realization shines in the cave of the heart.

How wonderful! He gave this remedy. To remain wonderful is how all the saints speak. Each one speaks in their own unique way.

We form groups by catching hold of saints, but the saints themselves live freely in their own independent ways, living, experiencing, and passing by beautifully.

That teacher says: "Maintain your body, cultivate your mind. Whatever you grow, I grow. If you grow something, I grow something. Your harvest is yours, my harvest is mine. But if you grow it yourself, you will be blissful."

That was the first disciple—the ordinary person—who listened to this and said, "This cultivation is wonderful, this is fine. Whatever you did outside is fine, but do this inner cultivation as well."

Maintaining the body, maintaining the inner instrument (*antahkarana*), which is called the heart or mind, it is a garden.
Cultivating that garden, making the mind steady, cutting through illusions and differences, breaking the ties of *samsara*, planting the seed of the supreme spirit—all of this is beautifully stated.

Everything is well-planted. Planting the seed of the supreme spirit—the unbroken circle, the breath moving through the *sushumna* nadi, restraining the senses, and then at that time, in your garden, awareness blossoms, and the moon is seen shining in the cave of the heart.

Wonderful! He gave a remedy: just make things wonderful, and he explains how to be wonderful. All saints speak like this; each has their own style, but they all point to the same truth.

We form groups around saints, but the saints themselves live independent, free lives, living beautifully and moving on.

That teacher says: "Nourish your body, nourish your mind. Cultivate your inner garden. If you grow your crop, I grow mine. Your crop is yours, mine is mine. But if you cultivate it yourself, you remain blissful."

That was the first disciple—the ordinary person—who listened and understood: "This cultivation is wonderful. Whatever you did outside is fine, but do this inner cultivation as well."

Maintaining the body, nurturing the inner instrument (*antahkarana*), the heart—it is a garden. Cultivating that garden, stabilizing the mind, cutting through all illusions and differences, breaking the bonds of *samsara*, and planting the seed of the supreme spirit—all of this is wonderfully explained.

Everything is well-established. Planting the seed of the supreme spirit—the unbroken circle, the breath moving through the *sushumna* nadi, restraining the senses, and then at that time, in your garden, awareness blossoms, and the moon is seen shining in the cave of the heart.

Wonderful! He gave a remedy: just make things wonderful, and he explains how to be wonderful. All saints speak like this; each has their own style, but they all point to the same truth.
