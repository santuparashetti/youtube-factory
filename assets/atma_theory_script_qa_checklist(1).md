# Atma Theory Script QA Checklist (Master)

Use this checklist before approving any script. The goal is to ensure
every script passes narrative, engagement, retention, and production QA
while staying within the **600--750 spoken-word** target.

## Pass Criteria

-   [ ] Spoken words: **600--750** (preferred **680--720**)
-   [ ] All **7 narrative beats** are clearly present.
-   [ ] All **5 engagement markers** are present.
-   [ ] Every beat is **PASS** (no WEAK/FAIL).
-   [ ] No redundant explanations.
-   [ ] Ending delivers emotional payoff before the subscribe CTA.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 1 -- Word Count

### Hard Rule

-   [ ] Spoken narration only is counted.
-   [ ] Ignore scene headings.
-   [ ] Ignore `Visual:` lines.
-   [ ] Ignore `Audio:` lines.
-   [ ] Ignore marker labels.
-   [ ] Ignore pause directions.

### Target

  Status         Spoken Words
  ------------ --------------
  Ideal              680--720
  Acceptable         600--750
  Fail              Below 600
  Fail              Above 750

### Auto-Fix

If over 750:

1.  Remove repetition.
2.  Compress transitions.
3.  Replace long explanations.
4.  Never remove a beat.
5.  Never remove engagement markers.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 2 -- Narrative Beat Checklist

### 1. DISRUPT

Purpose: Break the viewer's assumption.

-   [ ] Opens with a surprising reframe.
-   [ ] Creates curiosity immediately.
-   [ ] Viewer thinks: "I hadn't looked at it this way."

Example pattern:

> "You don't have a talent problem..."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2. CHALLENGE

Purpose: Make the viewer personally confront the problem.

-   [ ] Asks an uncomfortable question.
-   [ ] Creates personal tension.
-   [ ] Feels directed at the viewer.

Example:

> "What do you actually want to become?"

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3. PROVE

Purpose: Demonstrate the idea.

-   [ ] Uses at least one concrete story.
-   [ ] Shows cause → consequence.
-   [ ] Connects the story back to the thesis.

Examples:

-   Monkey story
-   Two Hills
-   Future scripts can use different stories.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4. REVEAL

Purpose: Deliver the central realization.

Must contain:

-   [ ] Old assumption.
-   [ ] New understanding.
-   [ ] Core insight.
-   [ ] Solution.

Formula:

> Old belief → Real problem → Core insight → Solution.

Example pattern:

> "The hill was never the problem..."

Avoid:

-   Jumping straight from problem to advice.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 5. FRAME

Purpose: Give a memorable mental model.

-   [ ] Two to four named principles.
-   [ ] Each principle is distinct.
-   [ ] Explains HOW to apply the reveal.

Example:

-   Identity First
-   Irrigation Mindset
-   Escape Test

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 6. APPLY

Purpose: Give an immediate action.

-   [ ] Starts with "Apply this today" or equivalent.
-   [ ] Specific action.
-   [ ] Can be completed today.
-   [ ] Reinforces the framework.

Example:

> "30 minutes. One promise."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 7. TRANSFORM

Purpose: Show who the viewer becomes.

Must include:

-   [ ] Before state.
-   [ ] After state.
-   [ ] Emotional payoff.

Formula:

> Before → After → Lasting identity shift.

Example:

> "You are someone who stays."

Avoid ending with advice only.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 3 -- Engagement Checklist

These markers are REQUIRED.

### 1. Value Promise

-   [ ] `*[ENGAGEMENT: value_promise]*`

Placed near the beginning.

Purpose:

> Tell viewers why they should keep watching.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2. Journey Invitation

-   [ ] `*[ENGAGEMENT: journey_invitation]*`

Placed after the first proof/story.

Purpose:

> Introduce the channel naturally.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3. Comment Prompt

-   [ ] `*[ENGAGEMENT: comment_prompt]*`

Placed before the narrative ending.

Rules:

-   [ ] Asks a specific question.
-   [ ] Prefer choosing between options.
-   [ ] Avoid generic "What do you think?"

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4. Subscribe Promise

-   [ ] `*[ENGAGEMENT: subscribe_promise]*`

Placed AFTER emotional payoff.

Good:

> "Subscribe so our next ancient idea finds you."

Avoid:

> "Don't forget to subscribe."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 5. Branding End

-   [ ] `*[ENGAGEMENT: branding_end]*`

Must be the final branding moment.

Example:

> "Atma Theory -- clarity for the restless mind."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 4 -- Structure Check

Preferred order:

``` text
HOOK
↓
VALUE PROMISE
↓
CORE CONFLICT
↓
PROOF
↓
JOURNEY INVITATION
↓
SECOND PROOF (optional)
↓
REVEAL
↓
FRAME
↓
APPLY
↓
TRANSFORM
↓
COMMENT
↓
NARRATIVE ENDING
↓
SUBSCRIBE
↓
BRANDING
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 5 -- Retention Check

### First 30 Seconds

-   [ ] Strong visual.
-   [ ] Unexpected statement.
-   [ ] Ancient wisdom meets modern problem.
-   [ ] Clear reason to continue.

### Middle

-   [ ] No repeated explanations.
-   [ ] Every story advances the thesis.
-   [ ] Transition naturally into the reveal.

### Ending

-   [ ] Emotional payoff.
-   [ ] Pause before final line.
-   [ ] Subscribe comes after value.
-   [ ] Branding closes the video.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 6 -- Language Rules

### Keep

-   Short sentences.
-   Strong verbs.
-   Cinematic imagery.
-   Second-person ("you").

### Avoid

-   Repeating the same idea twice.
-   Long philosophical paragraphs.
-   Productivity-jargon overload.
-   Multiple metaphors explaining the same point.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 7 -- Visual Sync Check

Every major beat should have a visual.

  Beat        Visual
  ----------- --------------------------
  Hook        Strong cinematic opening
  Proof       Story animation
  Reveal      Visual freeze/shift
  Frame       Simple visual labels
  Apply       Clear action
  Transform   Before/after contrast
  Ending      Final cinematic payoff

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 8 -- Auto-Fix Rules

If REVEAL is weak:

Replace existing sentences using:

> Old assumption → New understanding → Solution.

Do not add a new paragraph.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If TRANSFORM is weak:

Add:

-   Before state.
-   After state.
-   Emotional consequence.

Do not add unnecessary words.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If over word limit:

Priority:

1.  Compress repetition.
2.  Compress transitions.
3.  Shorten stories.
4.  Preserve every beat.
5.  Preserve every engagement marker.

Never solve word count by deleting the reveal or transformation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Engagement

-   [ ] Spoken words 600--750

-   [ ] No duplicate ideas
-   [ ] Ready for voice-over

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Quick 60-Second QA

Ask these ten questions:

1.  Would someone keep watching after 10 seconds?
2.  Is the problem personally relevant?
3.  Does the story prove the point?
4.  Is the reveal unmistakable?
5.  Can I remember the framework?
6.  Can I act today?
7.  Do I clearly become someone different?
8.  Are all engagement markers present?
9.  Is the spoken word count under 750?
10. Does the last emotional line stay with me after the video ends?

If every answer is **Yes**, the script is production-ready.
